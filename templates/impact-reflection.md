# Impact reflection guide

Use these questions when you are not sure what to write about a piece of work.
They can help with a working note, a design decision, or a broader project
reflection. Answer only what you know; skip questions that do not fit. “Unknown,”
“not measured,” and “not yet observed” are valid answers.

Keep answers public-safe. Do not include personal details, restricted research,
confidential plans, internal links, or unapproved metrics.

## Reflect on the work

1. **What was happening?** What project need, question, or goal were you working
   on? When was this, if known?
2. **What did you contribute?** What did you do, decide, explore, or help the
   team do? Separate your contribution from shared work.
3. **Why did it matter?** Which user need, project goal, or design constraint did
   the work relate to?
4. **What happened as a result of the work?** What did you deliver, inform,
   change, or help enable? Was a change in the experience or product observed?
5. **What supports that understanding?** What approved note, decision, research
   summary, observation, or impact record can you link?
6. **What does the evidence show—and not show?** What change was observed or
   measured? What is unknown, unmeasured, or based on interpretation? If using a
   metric, what are its units, period, source, and limitations? If the change is
   not quantified, what metric or evidence source could help find or measure it?
7. **What else influenced the result?** What team contributions, constraints, or
   other factors should be acknowledged? What would make you revise this
   reflection?

You do not need a measured result to describe meaningful work. If the change is
not known or measured, focus on your contribution and what evidence could be
found or collected to understand what happened. Do not predict an outcome.

## Draft an impact statement

Use the details you have to write one or two sentences in your own voice:

> I **[specific contribution]** to **[project need or goal]** by **[action,
> decision, or collaboration]**. **[Describe the observed change and evidence, if
> known; otherwise say what is not measured and what evidence could help quantify
> it.]** **[State what limits attribution, if relevant.]**

Choose wording that matches the evidence:

- For completed work, say what you delivered or contributed.
- For an observed outcome, name the evidence and its limits.
- Do not present goals, expectations, or hypothesized outcomes as impact. If a
  change is unmeasured, say so and suggest evidence to find or a metric to start
  tracking; do not predict a result.
- Prefer “contributed to” or “helped enable” when other people or factors also
  shaped the result. Do not claim causation from a before-and-after comparison
  alone.
- If there is no evidence of a change, describe the contribution without
  implying proven impact.

For a detailed evidence record or to identify a measure that could quantify a
change that happened, use the
[impact record template](impact.md). For a longer narrative, link the relevant
records in a [case study](case-study.md) or
[performance review](performance-review.md).

## Review before recording

- Does the statement sound like you and accurately describe your contribution?
- Can each substantive claim be linked to an approved source?
- Is the reflection about completed work, with observed changes and unknowns
  clearly distinguished?
- Have you kept team contributions and attribution limits visible?
- Is the content safe for this journal?

Save the reflection in the relevant note, decision, impact record, or output.
This guide is optional scaffolding, not a required form.
